# Case shortlist

Research pass of 14 Nov 2026 by a research agent, summarised. A claim here is a pointer for the dossier work, not a checked fact: each case's own dossier is where claims get verified against sources that were opened.

## How cases were judged

1. **Documented decisions.** Public, citable accounts of specific choices the campaign made and why.
2. **Data.** Results below borough level, dated finance filings, period demographics.
3. **Teaching value** across delegating, budgeting, reading voter groups and policy trade-offs, and variety across the five.
4. **Lower partisan heat.** Primaries preferred; the lesson is strategy, not which side was right.
5. **Settled record.**

## First deep case: 2013 Democratic mayoral primary

- **Best record.** Staff from every major campaign went on the record together afterwards (New School Center for New York City Affairs roundtable), alongside detailed newspaper reconstruction naming who argued for what.
- **All four skills in one arc.** The manager overruling the candidate on attacking a rival (delegating), television against mail under a spending cap (budgeting), contesting a rival's presumed base (voter groups), the hospital and policing stances (policy trade-offs).
- **Citywide and settled.** Uses the whole map; the record has been stable for years.
- **Finance data is excellent.** City filings give every dated expenditure with a purpose code, so the television-or-mail decision can be shown from the campaign's own books.

**Weak points**

- **Results geography.** The Board of Elections published 2013 only as PDFs by Assembly District. See `data-sources.md` for what that means for the map.
- **Luck.** Rivals' mistakes are part of every account of this race. The reveal has to say so.
- **Heat.** "Message beat the establishment" can read as ideological. Frame each decision as strategy.
- **A minor** appeared in the campaign's best-known ad. Refer to it factually and briefly.
