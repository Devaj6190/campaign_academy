# Why Campaign Academy matters: the evidence

This is the evidence for the pitch, README (#56) and Devpost (#59). Each figure links to its source. Read the notes before you quote anything.

**One rule for using it.** No study has measured Campaign Academy itself. Say "methods like ours raised X by Y". Don't say "Campaign Academy improves learning by Y".

## The full evidence, in three tiers

### Tier 1 · Direct: campaign and election simulations in classrooms

The closest match to what we built. These studies are small and often self-reported, so present them as supportive, not as proof.

- **Mock presidential election at Western Illinois University.** [Deitz & Boeckelman, *PS: Political Science & Politics* 45(4), 2012](https://www.cambridge.org/core/product/F783310ED1EAE570A37A3F56D6A543DE).
  - A year later, 47% of participants were "very interested" in politics, against 29% of non-participants (p < .001).
  - At the end, 82% said they were more likely to follow campaigns closely, and 42% more likely to volunteer or intern for a campaign.
  - Caveat: political efficacy was higher but not significantly. Participants chose to take part, so selection bias is possible.
- **Campaign budget simulation in Intro to American Government.** [Alberda, *PS* 49(4), 2016](https://www.cambridge.org/core/product/566A9A5DC75E72DAF0DC75E7B4516AC2).
  - Students rated it 4.5/5 for helping them learn about campaigns and elections.
  - Caveat: those ratings are self-reported. The difference in interest against the control section was not significant (p = .105).
  - The point for us: running a campaign budget is a recognised teaching method.
- **Live campaign simulation in an applied politics program.** [Coffey, Miller & Feuerstein, *Journal of Political Science Education* 7(1), 2011](https://www.editlib.org/p/50630).
  - Engagement rose, and the more engaged students gained more factual political knowledge.
  - Caveat: we read only the abstract.
- **The national civics framework.** The [Educating for American Democracy Roadmap](https://www.educatingforamericandemocracy.org/) names simulations among its core teaching principles. CIRCLE lists simulations among the practices of [high-quality civic learning](https://circle.tufts.edu/our-research/equitable-k12-civic-learning).

### Tier 2 · Adjacent: simulations, games, active learning and AI tutors

- **Simulation games in training.** [Sitzmann, *Personnel Psychology* 64, 2011](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/j.1744-6570.2011.01190.x): a meta-analysis of 65 studies with about 6,500 trainees.
  - Self-efficacy was **20% higher**, procedural knowledge **14%**, declarative knowledge **11%** and retention **9%**, against comparison groups.
  - Effects were larger when the game was active and when it was combined with other instruction. Campaign Academy has both: you direct the staff, and a debrief follows.
  - Caveat: when the comparison group had *actively engaging* instruction, the comparison group did better. Don't claim simulations beat every method.
- **Serious games.** [Wouters et al., *Journal of Educational Psychology* 105(2), 2013](https://doi.org/10.1037/a0031311).
  - Games beat conventional instruction on learning (d = 0.29) and retention (d = 0.36).
  - Effects were larger across several sessions and when combined with other instruction. Campaign Academy is played over six decisions and ends with a debrief.
  - Caveat: the games were **not** more motivating. Don't cite this study for engagement.
- **Active learning.** [Freeman et al., PNAS 2014](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4060654/): see row 5 above.
  - Caveat: undergraduate STEM only, and "lecture" was defined narrowly.
- **The feeling of learning misleads.** [Deslauriers et al., PNAS 2019](https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.1821936116).
  - In a Harvard randomized trial, students in active learning learned more but *felt* they learned less.
  - Useful when someone asks why the tool makes students decide before it shows the answer.
- **AI tutoring.** [Kestin et al., Scientific Reports 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12179260/): see row 6 above.
  - The effect size was 0.63 SD on the conservative estimate.
  - Caveat: a structured physics tutor, 194 Harvard students, two lessons, no delayed test. Our AI staff are role-play agents, not tutors, so this is adjacent evidence.

### Tier 3 · Need: why it matters now

- **Civic knowledge.**
  - [NAEP 2022](https://nces.ed.gov/nationsreportcard/civics/): 22% proficient. The "first decline" is the average score (153 to 150), not the proficient share, which is flat.
  - [Annenberg 2026](https://www.annenbergpublicpolicycenter.org/a-third-of-americans-cannot-name-the-three-branches-of-government/): 35% can't name the branches. The 2025 figure was better (70% could name them), so say the 2026 result is "unchanged", not "getting worse".
- **Youth turnout is high but uneven.**
  - [CIRCLE](https://circle.tufts.edu/latest-research/new-data-nearly-half-youth-voted-2024): 47% of 18–29s voted in 2024 (50% in 2020). Turnout was 41% for 18–19-year-olds. By race it was 55% for white youth, against 34% for Black and 32% for Latino youth.
- **NYC 2025.**
  - The CFB puts 18–29 general-election turnout at 41.9%, from city records. CIRCLE's exit-poll estimate [is about 28%](https://circle.tufts.edu/latest-research/young-voters-power-mamdani-victory-shape-key-2025-elections). The two use different methods, so name the source when you quote either.
  - Nearly a quarter of early primary voters [had never voted in a Democratic primary](https://gothamist.com/news/nearly-a-quarter-of-nycs-early-voters-hadnt-voted-in-a-democratic-primary-since-2012).
- **Too few people run, and the contests aren't real.**
  - [Ballotpedia 2024](https://news.ballotpedia.org/2024/09/27/ballotpedia-releases-14th-annual-state-legislative-competitiveness-report/): **38% of state legislative seats** (2,227) had no major-party competition.
  - Only **16% of primaries** were contested.
  - The point for us: the bottleneck is not just voters. Few people know how to run.
- **Trust.**
  - [Harvard Youth Poll, Spring 2026](https://iop.harvard.edu/news/new-poll-just-13-youth-feel-america-headed-right-direction): only 15% of young Americans trust the federal government to do the right thing most of the time.
  - Only 33% expect the 2026 midterms to be run fairly.
  - The point for us: seeing a real campaign from the inside, with sources, is an antidote to cynicism. That is an argument, not a measured result.
- **Funding.**
  - A 2019 estimate put federal spending at about [$54 per student on STEM and $0.05 on civics](https://defense360.csis.org/bad-idea-prioritizing-stem-education-at-the-expense-of-civic-education/).
  - Funding has risen since, with [$150M+ in civics grants in Oct 2025 (NPR)](https://www.npr.org/2025/10/22/nx-s1-5547196/education-department-pours-over-150-million-into-civics-training-for-k-12-teachers). If you use the ratio, date it as 2019.

## A one-line pitch the evidence supports

> Only 22% of eighth-graders are proficient in civics, and 38% of state legislative seats go without a real contest. Students learn more by doing than by listening (Freeman, PNAS), and campaign simulations raise political interest (47% vs 29%, Deitz & Boeckelman). Campaign Academy lets students do it inside a real, sourced race.

## Don't claim

- That Campaign Academy has been shown to improve learning. It hasn't been tested yet.
- That simulations beat *every* method (Sitzmann's comparison groups with active instruction did better), or that games are more motivating (Wouters).
- That civic knowledge is "falling fast". NAEP's proficient share is flat, and Annenberg is unchanged.
